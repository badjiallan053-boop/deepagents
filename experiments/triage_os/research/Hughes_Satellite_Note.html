01Executive summary

Our view

The secured notes are the better-protected claim, and the swing factor for them is subordination, not enterprise value. If the $172.7m of EchoStar-held secured claims (the $122.7m of notes it bought in 2025 plus the $50m J3 secured advance) is subordinated, third-party secured holders share the collateral with nobody. Under our base case that moves their recovery from about 64 to 84 cents. The unsecured notes, at a fair value of 58.0 on 30 Jun 2026, are a claim on a litigation trust that does not yet exist.

  • What happened. Hughes did not pay $1.5bn of notes that matured on 1 Aug 2026, a Saturday, and filed for Chapter 11 the next day[15][1]. No DIP proposal had been received as of the 19 Aug deposition[24], and the case is funded with cash collateral[32]. EchoStar is the parent and the J3 satellite lessor, and it also holds $122.7m of the secured notes[4][5].
  • The fight. A Noteholder Group holding more than 61.6% of the secured notes and more than 84.37% of the unsecured notes has moved to terminate exclusivity and file its own debt-for-equity plan. That plan would put the estate's claims against EchoStar into a litigation trust[2][19]. On 21 Oct the court refused, on an interim basis, to strike that motion and set a hearing for 1 Dec 2026 at 9:00 AM[27].
  • Why it matters. The Group alleges that EchoStar took $1.029bn of dividends and $196m of tax reimbursements in 2024, and that the J3 lease is above market[13]. These are allegations, not findings. Whoever controls the plan controls whether they are prosecuted, settled or released.
  • Liquidity is the clock. The Group alleges there is no unencumbered cash, about $48m of J3 payments deferred and about $43m of estate professional fees deferred[26][25]. Hughes had $119m of cash and current marketable securities at FY2025[28].

Operating backdrop

Dkt 4 ¶¶ (first-day declaration)[9].

FY2025 revenue was about $1.4bn[10], against a net loss of about $1.274bn, which was driven largely by consumer broadband revenue declines and a large non-cash charge[11]. These are TRIAGE calculations from assumptions A1–A5 and are not estimates of value. Cents are per $ of claim. Third-party secured holders are $627.3m. The unsecured pool is $774.8m plus any secured deficiency. "Incl. trust" adds each secured holder's share of trust proceeds on its deficiency claim. The sum is capped at 100.

Reading the grid. In this model, if the business is worth about $600m and EchoStar's claims are subordinated, third-party secured holders recover about 84 cents, close to their reported 85.6. On a pari passu basis the same value gives roughly 64 cents. That is the gap the Group is fighting over. For the unsecured notes to be worth their reported 58.0, the trust would need to deliver about $449m net, with secured holders paid in full first. That is well above our base case. Their price therefore embeds a large litigation recovery, a settlement premium from EchoStar, or both.

06Plan-fight dynamics

Debtors / EchoStar

  • Exclusivity keeps plan design, and with it the treatment of claims against the parent, with a board the parent controls. The Group says the controlling shareholder holds about 90.4% of EchoStar's votes[31].
  • EchoStar is landlord (J3, $15.9m a month)[7], secured lender ($50m at 13.75%)[8] and holds $122.7m of the secured notes[5].
  • Running on cash collateral[32] means there is no DIP lender to impose milestones[24].

Noteholder Group

  • Its plan is a standalone debt-for-equity deal. Reorganized equity and new secured debt go to unaffiliated secured holders, and a sale process closes within a year[17][22].
  • Claims against EchoStar go into a litigation trust. The unsecured notes, about $774.8m, recover from it[19]. Its tiers accrue 13.75%, the same rate EchoStar charges Hughes[20].
  • The J3 lease would be rejected unless EchoStar XXIV agrees to modify it[21]. A member would provide a bridge DIP[29].

What the 1 December court has to weigh

To end exclusivity "for cause" under §1121(d), the court will look at whether a controlled debtor can credibly deal at arm's length with its controlling parent. It will also weigh the creditor concentration: the Group holds 73.71% of the secured notes excluding EchoStar[3] and more than 84.37% of the unsecured notes[2]. The question is whether the Debtors have shown reasonable prospects of filing a confirmable plan. A plain denial of the motion to strike keeps the termination motion alive, but it does not grant it[27]. Possible outcomes include:

  • Termination granted. The Group files its plan within 20 days. Disclosure-statement approval follows within about 40 days, then confirmation about 30 days later[22]. The fight then shifts to subordination and the J3 lease.
  • Exclusivity shortened or conditioned. The most common middle path. It gives the Debtors a deadline and creates settlement pressure on EchoStar.
  • Motion denied. The Debtors keep control. Watch for a parent-sponsored plan that includes releases, and for an examiner or standing motion.

08Risks to the view

  • Valuation. Low-earth-orbit competition could push the operating value below our $350m low case. Secured recovery would then fall with collateral value, whichever way subordination goes.
  • Subordination is a litigated outcome, not a given. Equitable subordination requires inequitable conduct. EchoStar may defend its $122.7m of notes as open-market purchases[4].
  • Allegations may fail. The dividend, tax and lease claims are the Group's characterisations[13][14]. A low trust recovery means unsecured value stays near our low case.
  • Settlement. A parent settlement could release the claims for a fixed payment. This compresses outcomes for both classes.
  • Data limits. Fair values are the issuer's Level 2 estimates, not traded prices. The Dkt 93 member list was not retrieved. Trade claims are not quantified.
