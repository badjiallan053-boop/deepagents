TRIAGE Edge Lab

How the big funds find edge, and what survives our tests on free public data
Mechanisms10 tested or scoped, 1 kept
DataEDGAR idx 2018–2026, Form 4 bulk, 8-K full text
RulesPIT filing dates · costs · OOS ≥ 2022

As of 23 Oct 2026 (SGT). Public data, research only, not investment advice, paper ledger only. A grade means a primary document was read this pass; B means a book or the fund's own filings; C means secondary or judgement.

Bottom line. Of seven mechanisms we could rebuild from free SEC data, one survives our rules (late-filer SHORT/AVOID). It is hard to trade because borrow is scarce. Spin-offs are FRAGILE, post-reorganisation equity is UNTESTED (too few clean events), and insider clusters, activist 13Ds and lockup shorts are KILLED. Merger arb, earnings drift and fallen angels need paid data.

How to read this. - Means are skewed by a few huge winners; check the medians. - Spin-off OOS mean +46% vs median +4% is a handful of multi-baggers, not a reliable edge. In-sample precision of 47% makes it FRAGILE. - Late-filer short: borrow cost decides it. OOS excess after a 100bp cost: 14.93%. After borrow at 5% / 20% / 50% a year: 5%/yr: 12.53%, 20%/yr: 5.33%, 50%/yr: -9.07%. Most of these names are sub-$1, often not borrowable. Treat it as an AVOID / risk signal, not a trade. - Survivorship: Yahoo drops delisted tickers. Coverage is 68–93%. Missing names are disproportionately failures, so long results are biased UP and the short result is biased DOWN (conservative). - Insider clusters killed: the academic edge (Cohen-Malloy-Pomorski) uses 'opportunistic' insiders and pre-2010 data. Our simple ≥3-insider rule over 2018–2026 loses money vs IWM after 50bp. - Lockup short: worked in-sample (t = 2.55) and failed OOS. It looks arbitraged away, as the literature predicts. - Post-reorg equity: the 'new equity' filter leaves 13 clean events. Our EDGAR text-search dating is too coarse; it needs court confirmation dates (our Ch11 data) to reach n ≥ 8 OOS.
