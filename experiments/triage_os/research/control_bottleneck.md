# Control bottleneck

## 1. The answer in one page

**Hughes (26-90739) has one chokepoint, and it is already owned.** The Noteholder Group's disclosed positions (more than 61.6% of the secured notes, more than 84.37% of the unsecured notes) give it, simultaneously:

- the **blocking and carrying vote in both note classes** under §1126(c)[CB01][CB02];
- the **cash-collateral trigger**: the "Required Prepetition Secured Parties" are the majority of secured notes, and EchoStar XXIV is named without a vote[CB05]. The order runs only through **23 Oct 2026**[CB06], so the Group re-prices the Debtors' liquidity every few weeks;
- a **veto on any new EchoStar/Debtor transaction** and on settling estate claims outside mediation, under the 20 Sep stipulated order[CB11][CB12].

The float the Group does not own is too small to block either class. Non-Group third parties hold at most **$165.3m of the secured notes** and **$117.2m of the unsecured notes**. Blocking needs more than $209.1m and $250.0m respectively. **An outsider cannot buy a blocking position in the market. It can only buy one from Group members, or join the Group.**

The counter-chokepoint belongs to **EchoStar (the parent)**. It owns the J3 satellite through non-debtor EchoStar XXIV, holds $172.7m of secured claims, and controls the Debtors' board until exclusivity ends. Its claims are the target of the examiner's equitable-subordination brief[CB08][CB10]. As of 16 Oct, though, the order recites that **no examiner had yet been appointed**[CB07]. The three dates that decide control are **23 Oct** (cash collateral), **1 Dec** (exclusivity hearing) and the **lien-challenge deadline**.

**What a billionaire would do (judgement):** not fight the Group. Instead, (1) buy into it through a member's position, or (2) take the one seat it does not hold: **litigation-trust funder** or **bidder for the post-exit equity / sale process**. In the side situations the same logic gives:
- **Fresno**: about $11.2m of CMBS class F controls the directing seat on a $325m loan.
- **Branicks**: more than 50% of a €400m bond can waive the broken shareholder condition.
- **BayWa**: more than 25% of the votes cast blocks the hybrid.
- **Loparex**: the lenders who led the out-of-court recapitalisation already own the outcome.

## 2. Hughes: voting math, class by class

The rule is §1126(c): a class accepts if creditors holding **at least two-thirds in amount and more than half in number of the claims that actually vote** accept. Votes designated under §1126(e) are excluded (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1126). A plan also needs one impaired accepting class, **counted without insider votes**[CB14].

Prices are the issuer's reported fair values at 30 Jun 2026: **85.6** secured, **58.0** unsecured[CB03][CB04]. These are Level-2 estimates, not trades. Group holdings are the disclosed minimums.

**How to read it.**
- **Secured class.** EchoStar's $122.7m is 16.4% of the class and cannot block alone in amount. If EchoStar is designated, as DISH was in DBSD (below), the class shrinks to $627.3m. The Group then holds 73.71% and carries the class by itself[CB02]. **But if EchoStar is *not* designated, the Group (≥$462.0m) falls short of the $500m needed to carry the full class alone. EchoStar ($122.7m) plus at least about $127.3m of the ≤$165.3m non-Group float (more than $250m in total) could block. That makes designation of EchoStar's vote the real swing in the secured class, and makes the remaining non-Group secured holders the kingmakers (judgement).**
- **Numerosity is the only soft spot.** "More than half in number" counts holders, not dollars. The member count on Exhibit A of ECF 154 was **not retrievable** (not on RECAP; the Epiq site needs JavaScript)[CB13]. A plan sponsor facing a Group of fewer than about 10 funds could in theory be out-voted by number if many small holders voted against it. Judgement: this is unlikely for institutional 144A notes, where holders vote through DTC participants and turnout among small holders is low.
- **Turnout lowers every threshold.** If only 70% of the secured class votes, blocking needs just $175.0m. The Group's position only gets more dominant.
- **EchoStar intercompany class.** EchoStar can make this class reject (cost: nothing, it owns the claim). But its acceptance cannot satisfy §1129(a)(10)[CB14], and a rejecting class can be crammed down if the plan is fair and equitable. Its real leverage is economic, not legal: **J3**. The satellite sits outside the estate. The Group plan rejects the lease unless EchoStar XXIV modifies it, so Hughes needs replacement capacity or a renegotiated lease.

**Legal constraints common to all five.**
- **MNPI.** Group members sign NDAs and trade behind walls; an outsider buying from them must get them "cleansed". Trading on Debtor or EchoStar MNPI violates Rule 10b-5.
- **Rule 2019.** Anyone acting in concert must disclose every "disclosable economic interest", including derivatives[CB17].
- **§1126(e).** Votes bought to obtain a non-creditor advantage can be designated (DBSD).
- **Insider status.** Anyone affiliated with EchoStar cannot supply the impaired accepting class[CB14].

**Ranking (judgement).** Highest risk-adjusted control: **(b) the litigation trust**, because it is the one valuable seat the Group does not already own. Next is **(e)**, but only as a partner of the Group's sale process. **(c)** works only through a Group member. **(a)** is effectively unavailable. **(d)** is yield, not control.

## 5. Fresno Fashion Fair (CMBS): the B-piece holds the pen

- **The loan.** Fresno Fashion Fair is a $325m whole loan; the $60m A-1-A note sits in JPMDB 2016-C4 at 3.587% and matures 1 Nov 2026[CB19]. It moved to special servicing on 29 Jul 2026 for imminent monetary default. Rialto, the special servicer, is finalising a Pre-Negotiation Letter with the sponsor, Macerich[CB20].
- **Who controls.**
  - The trust is the controlling noteholder for the whole loan.
  - The **Directing Certificateholder is Cytium Investment Management**[CB21]. At issuance it was BlackRock Realty Advisors, which bought Classes E, F and NR and appointed the special servicer[CB22].
  - The controlling class is **F: $11.244m face, now receiving zero interest**.
- **The chokepoint math.** About **$11.2m of face directs workout, extension or foreclosure consent on a $325m loan: a 29x lever**. The holder can also replace the special servicer, subject to appraisal-reduction control-shift tests. Judgement: if appraisal reductions erase class F, control shifts up to class E ($22.5m), so a buyer must model the appraisal.
- **Billionaire move (assumption).** Buy class F + E (about $34m face) at deep discount from the current holder, then drive a negotiated extension with Macerich. The alternative is to buy the companion notes held in other trusts, which carry no control.

## 6. Loparex: control already exercised out of court

- **The lender's position.** OBDC holds second-lien positions of $112m and $21m, marked 61 → 5. Its first-lien pieces are small: $4.12m at 21.5 and $0.78m at 102.6 (30 Jun 2026).
- **The recap.** Loparex announced a recapitalisation on 8 Sep 2026, **led by Monarch Alternative Capital and Atlantic Park**, expected to close in Q4 2026. https://loparex.com/loparex-announces-comprehensive-recapitalization-and-strategic-capital-support-to-advance-next-stage-of-growth/
- **Second-lien and first-lien treatment.** Secondary press reports the second lien goes to zero and about half of the first lien converts to equity. **This is not confirmed by a primary source.**
- **No WHOA filing was found.** The chokepoint was the majority-lender consent under the first-lien credit agreement. It has been used, and the BDC 2L holders were not on the deciding side.
- **Lesson.** In private credit, control sits with whoever holds the required-lender percentage of the senior tranche, not with the largest BDC mark.

## 7. BayWa (Kronos EKS €100m hybrid): the 25% blocker

- **The rule.** Under §5(4) SchVG, material amendments need **75% of votes cast**. The first vote needs a quorum of 50% of outstanding notes (§15(3)); the second meeting needs 25%. https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/schvg/__5.html https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/schvg/__15.html
- **The October vote.** The 12–14 Oct vote was **quorate, yet neither the issuer's resolutions nor the countermotions passed**[CB23].
- **Minimum blocking stake.** At minimum participation (50 of €100m), more than **€12.5m** blocks. At full participation, more than €25m.
- **Second meeting.** At its 25% quorum, as little as **€6.25m** can block.
- **Judgement.** A €6–15m holder, buying hybrids at distressed prices, has a veto on a €100m write-down. That is the cheapest control seat in this set.

## 8. Branicks: the 50% waiver and the shareholder veto

- **What creditors approved.** Creditors approved the restructuring of the €400m 2.25% bond (M05: 99% of votes cast).
- **What shareholders blocked.** The 9 Oct EGM **did not approve** the consultancy agreements (agenda item 4), so Implementation Condition 2 failed[CB24].
- **The way around it.** Holders of **more than 50% of the outstanding principal (>€200m)** can instruct MR Treuhand to waive any condition except 6 and 7[CB25].
- **Where the chokepoint sits.** It is a **€200m+ coalition of noteholders**, not the shareholders.
- **Judgement.** The anchor holders who negotiated the deal (not named in the primary text) already sit close to that line. An outsider with 10–15% can bargain for consideration but cannot stop a waiver.
