# Where the next dislocation is

## 1. The answer

**The most likely $10bn+ dislocation is in private-credit liquidity, not in a single credit.** Seven large non-traded BDCs promise quarterly liquidity of about 5% of shares. In their latest completed rounds, holders asked for **$13.8bn** and **$8.5bn went unmet**. That is a single quarter at six funds; the queue rolls forward because unfilled requests are resubmitted.

The second-ranked thesis is the **CRE maturity wall into office**. CMBS delinquency is 8.02%, the highest since November 2020 [SR01], office is 12.16% [SR02], and office values are 33% below peak [SR03], against $875bn of 2026 and $652bn of 2027 commercial-mortgage maturities [SR05].

The two are linked. Insurers hold ~$1.2tn of private credit [SR07]. Banks lend to the funds and to CRE: 449 banks run CRE above 300% of capital.

| 3 | CRE-concentrated community and regional banks | 449 banks above 300% CRE/capital, $1,067bn of assets | Medium (30–45%, judgement) of a cluster of failures or forced loan sales above $10bn | Loan-sale buyers with bank-quality underwriting; larger regionals in FDIC-assisted deals (judgement) |
| 4 | Insurer private-credit and Schedule BA concentration | $1.2tn private-credit exposure [SR07]; $637.9bn Schedule BA [SR08] | Low-medium (15–25%, judgement) of a $10bn+ forced-sale event within 18 months | Reinsurers and sidecars; multi-strategy credit funds (judgement) |
| 5 | Fund-overlap cascades in stressed issuers (EchoStar/DISH complex) | ~$4bn of DISH DBS + Hughes debt held across 300+ registered funds (N-PORT, NAV-weighted) | Medium for idiosyncratic $1–5bn moves; low for $10bn+ (judgement) | Event-driven and distressed funds with no ratings constraint (judgement) |

**What argues against a near-term systemic break (counter-evidence, from the same data):**
- **High-yield spreads are tight** at 3.27% OAS (2026-10-21).
- **Banks are easing, not tightening, CRE standards.** SLOOS net tightening is NFNR -11.3 and multifamily -5.7.
- **Bank CRE credit is still benign.** The delinquency rate is 1.53% and the charge-off rate 0.14%.
- **Prices are up.** Green Street all-property values are +4.7% year on year [SR04].
- **Defaults are expected to fall.** Moody's projects lower default rates by December [SR12].

The dislocations ranked here are therefore **liquidity and refinancing events in specific pools of capital**, not a broad credit crisis (judgement).

## 8. Cross-border and rest of world

- **China.** About 0.6% of ~$150bn of defaulted developer offshore debt has been recovered in cash (secondary source) [SR16]. The dislocation has already been realised; the open question is the recovery-trading opportunity, not contagion.
- **Defaults.** The Moody's spec-grade default rate was 4.5% globally and 5.3% in the US in August 2026 [SR12]. Form PF private-fund gross assets were $29.6tn in 2025Q4 [SR15], with no private-credit breakout in public statistics.
